MODULE 1: SCALES AND KEY SIGNATURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Definition of a Scale:
- An orderly stepwise progression or alphabetical succession of musical pitches, ascending or descending within an octave.

1.2 Major Scale Formula:
- The standard interval pattern of a Major Scale: Tone - Tone - Semitone - Tone - Tone - Tone - Semitone (T - T - S - T - T - T - S).
- Semitones naturally fall between the 3rd & 4th degrees and the 7th & 8th degrees.

1.3 Key Signatures of Common Major Scales:
- C Major: Has 0 sharps and 0 flats (Notes: C - D - E - F - G - A - B - C).
- G Major: Has 1 sharp on F (F#) (Notes: G - A - B - C - D - E - F# - G).
- D Major: Has 2 sharps on F and C (F#, C#) (Notes: D - E - F# - G - A - B - C# - D).
- F Major: Has 1 flat on B (Bb) (Notes: F - G - A - Bb - C - D - E - F).

MODULE 2: MUSICAL INTERVALS AND TRIADS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Musical Intervals:
- An interval is the difference in pitch (distance) between two musical notes.
- Types:
  * Melodic Interval: The two notes are played or sung successively (one after another).
  * Harmonic Interval: The two notes are sounded simultaneously (at the exact same time).
- Numerical Distance: 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th, 6th, 7th, Octave (8th).
- Quality: Perfect (Unison, 4th, 5th, Octave), Major, Minor, Augmented, Diminished.

2.2 The Tonic Triad (Chord I):
- A chord consisting of 3 notes stacked in thirds: Root (1st / Doh), Third (3rd / Me), and Fifth (5th / Soh).
- Example in C Major: C - E - G.

MODULE 3: WESTERN MUSICAL INSTRUMENTS & THE ORCHESTRA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The four main instrumental families of the Symphony Orchestra:
1. Strings (Bowed or plucked strings):
   - Violin (highest pitch), Viola, Violoncello (Cello), Double Bass (lowest pitch), and Harp.
2. Woodwinds (Blown pipes, formerly made of wood; sound created with reeds or blowholes):
   - Flute & Piccolo (no reed, edge-blown)
   - Clarinet & Saxophone (single vibrating reed)
   - Oboe & English Horn (double vibrating reed)
   - Bassoon & Contrabassoon (double reed, deep bass register)
3. Brass (Cup-mouthpiece metal horns; sound made by buzzing lips):
   - Trumpet (highest pitch, bright tone)
   - French Horn (mellow, coiled horn)
   - Trombone (uses a sliding mechanism to alter tube length)
   - Tuba (lowest and heaviest brass instrument)
4. Percussion (Struck, shaken, or scraped):
   - Tuned / Definite Pitch: Timpani (Kettle drums), Xylophone, Glockenspiel, Tubular Bells.
   - Untuned / Indefinite Pitch: Snare Drum, Bass Drum, Cymbals, Triangle, Tambourine.

MODULE 4: ERAS IN WESTERN MUSIC HISTORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Baroque Era (c. 1600 – 1750):
   - Characteristics: Polyphonic texture, continuous bass (Basso Continuo), ornate melodies, terraced dynamics.
   - Prominent Composers: Johann Sebastian Bach, George Frideric Handel, Antonio Vivaldi.
2. Classical Era (c. 1750 – 1820):
   - Characteristics: Homophonic texture (melody + chordal accompaniment), clarity, balance, symmetry, development of the Sonata-Allegro form and symphony orchestra.
   - Prominent Composers: Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, Franz Joseph Haydn, Ludwig van Beethoven (early/middle).
3. Romantic Era (c. 1820 – 1900):
   - Characteristics: Intense emotional expression, nationalism, expanded orchestra, dramatic programmatic music.
   - Prominent Composers: Frédéric Chopin, Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky, Johannes Brahms, Franz Liszt.

MODULE 5: CONTEMPORARY AND POPULAR NIGERIAN MUSIC GENRES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Highlife:
   - Originated in West Africa in early 20th century. Characterized by jazzy horn riffs, syncopated guitar rhythms, and indigenous percussion.
   - Pioneers: E.T. Mensah, Cardinal Rex Lawson, Victor Olaiya, Chief Osita Osadebe, Oliver de Coque.
2. Juju Music:
   - Yoruba fusion of traditional percussion (talking drums) with Western guitars, accordion, and praise singing.
   - Pioneers: Tunde Nightingale, King Sunny Ade (synths & pedal steel guitar), Chief Commander Ebenezer Obey.
3. Afrobeat:
   - Powerful fusion of jazz, funk, psychedelic rock, traditional Yoruba highlife, and political activism.
   - Pioneer: Fela Anikulapo Kuti and his band Africa 70 / Egypt 80 (featuring drummer Tony Allen).
4. Fuji Music:
   - Evolved from the Islamic morning wake-up song tradition (Were/Ajisari). Features dense talking drums, Sakara, and vocal storytelling without Western guitars.
   - Pioneers: Alhaji Sikiru Ayinde Barrister, Ayinla Kollington, King Wasiu Ayinde Marshal (KWAM 1).
5. Apala Music:
   - Acoustic Yoruba folk genre using Sekere, Agidigbo (thumb piano), and Akuba drums.
   - Pioneer: Haruna Ishola.

MODULE 6: MUSICAL TERMS AND DYNAMICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Dynamics (Volume Markings):
- Pianissimo (pp): Very soft
- Piano (p): Soft
- Mezzo-piano (mp): Moderately soft
- Mezzo-forte (mf): Moderately loud
- Forte (f): Loud
- Fortissimo (ff): Very loud
- Crescendo (<): Gradually getting louder
- Decrescendo / Diminuendo (>): Gradually getting softer

6.2 Tempo (Speed Markings):
- Largo: Very slow and broad
- Adagio: Slow and stately
- Andante: At a walking pace
- Moderato: Moderate speed
- Allegro: Fast, lively, and bright
- Presto: Very fast
- Accelerando (accel.): Gradually speeding up
- Rallentando / Ritardando (rall. / rit.): Gradually slowing down
