MODULE 1: ADVANCED HARMONY AND MUSICAL CADENCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Four-Part Harmony (SATB):
- Soprano (Treble voice), Alto (Female low voice), Tenor (Male high voice), Bass (Male lowest root voice).
- Voice Leading: Avoiding parallel fifths and octaves to ensure independent musical voice lines.

1.2 Cadences (Musical Punctuation at the ends of phrases):
- Perfect Cadence (Full Close): Chord V moving to Chord I (Dominant -> Tonic). Sounds completely final and finished (like a period in grammar).
- Plagal Cadence (Amen Close): Chord IV moving to Chord I (Subdominant -> Tonic). Frequently used for singing "Amen" in choral music.
- Imperfect Cadence (Half Close): Any chord (I, II, IV, or VI) moving to Chord V. Sounds incomplete, expectant, and unfinished (like a comma).
- Interrupted / Deceptive Cadence: Chord V moving to Chord VI (Submediant). Surprises the listener by not resolving to Tonic Chord I.

MODULE 2: TRANSPOSITION AND MODULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Transposition:
- The process of rewriting or performing a piece of music in a different key (higher or lower in pitch) while maintaining all relative interval relationships.
- Practical need: Adjusting vocal pieces to suit a singer's comfortable voice range.

2.2 Modulation:
- The smooth transition or change from one key (tonality) to another within the same continuous musical composition.

MODULE 3: AFRICAN MUSICAL FORMS AND STRUCTURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Key Structural Characteristics:
- Call-and-Response: A lead singer or solo instrument chants a musical phrase ("call"), followed by a choral or instrumental group repetition / answering phrase ("response").
- Ostinato: A persistently repeated short melodic or rhythmic pattern that serves as the rhythmic foundation for improvisation.
- Polyrhythm & Cross-Rhythms: The simultaneous occurrence of two or more contrasting rhythmic meters (e.g., 3 against 2, or 3 against 4) creating complex layered interlocking textures.
- Syncopation: Deliberate shifting of the musical accent from the expected strong beat to a weak beat or offbeat.
- Pentatonic Scale: Five-tone scale system (e.g., Doh-Ray-Me-Soh-Lah) prevalent in indigenous African folk melodies.

MODULE 4: PROMINENT NIGERIAN AND AFRICAN ART COMPOSERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Fela Sowande (1905 – 1987):
   - Celebrated as the Father of Modern Nigerian Art Music.
   - Master organist, composer, and musicologist. Notable work: "African Suite for String Orchestra" combining Nigerian folk themes with Western classical counterpoint.
2. Thomas King Ekundayo (T.K.E.) Phillips (1884 – 1969):
   - Regarded as the Father of Nigerian Church Music.
   - Organist and choirmaster at Cathedral Church of Christ, Lagos. Authored foundational treatises on Yoruba church music.
3. Dayo Dedeke:
   - Celebrated composer of indigenous Yoruba choral anthems and educational school songs (e.g., "Ma Gbàgbé Ilé", "Ise Ni Oogun Ise").
4. Prof. Lazarus (Laz) Ekwueme (b. 1936):
   - Distinguished scholar, choral director, musicologist, and opera singer. Founder of the Laz Ekwueme National Chorale.
5. Fela Anikulapo Kuti (1938 – 1997):
   - Multi-instrumentalist, musical visionary, and originator of the Afrobeat genre and the Afrika Shrine.

MODULE 5: MUSIC TECHNOLOGY, ACOUSTICS, AND DIGITAL AUDIO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Sound and Acoustics:
- Frequency: Number of sound vibrations per second, measured in Hertz (Hz). Dictates pitch.
- Amplitude: Magnitude of sound wave displacement, measured in Decibels (dB). Dictates volume/loudness.

5.2 Digital Audio Systems:
- Transducer: A device that converts physical sound waves into electrical signals (e.g., Microphone) or electrical signals into sound waves (e.g., Loudspeaker/Headphones).
- Audio Interface (Sound Card): Converts analog audio signals to digital binary data (Analog-to-Digital Converter - ADC) and vice versa (DAC).
- MIDI (Musical Instrument Digital Interface): A universal protocol that transmits note, pitch, velocity, and control commands between electronic musical instruments and computers (transmits data, not audio).
- DAW (Digital Audio Workstation): Specialized software used for recording, editing, sequencing, arranging, and mixing music tracks (e.g., FL Studio, Logic Pro, Pro Tools, Ableton Live, GarageBand).

MODULE 6: CAREERS IN MUSIC AND INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY / COPYRIGHT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Music Industry Career Paths:
- Music Producer: Oversees the artistic direction, arrangement, and technical production of recorded music.
- Sound Engineer (Mixing / Mastering Engineer): Controls recording levels, audio processing, equalization, and final sonic balance.
- Musicologist / Ethnomusicologist: Academic researcher studying the cultural and historical aspects of music.
- Jingle Writer / Film Scorer: Creates commercial audio advertisements and cinematic soundtracks.

6.2 Copyright and Intellectual Property:
- Copyright: Legal ownership granting creators exclusive rights to reproduce, perform, distribute, and broadcast their original musical works.
- Royalties: Financial compensation paid to composers, songwriters, and performers whenever their copyrighted music is broadcast, streamed, or performed publicly.
- Copyright Society of Nigeria (COSON) & MCSN: Collective management organizations responsible for licensing and collecting music royalties for Nigerian artists.
- Anti-Piracy: Protecting artistic works against unauthorized distribution, bootlegging, and illegal digital downloads.
